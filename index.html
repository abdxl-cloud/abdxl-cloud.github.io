✨ What Is flunkey?

flunkey is a voice-first productivity tool built for people who think faster than they type. It is an open source alternative to Wisprflow, created for anyone who wants to stop pecking at a keyboard and start talking to their computer instead. You hold a key, you speak, and the words appear in whatever app you are already using — a browser, an email client, a chat window, a document, or a notes app.

Everything runs from a small, lightweight desktop application. There is no complicated setup, no server to configure, and no account to create before you can start. You download it, you run it, and you talk. That is the whole idea.

Behind the scenes, flunkey pairs modern speech recognition with smart text clean-up, so what lands on your screen reads like something you would have typed — not a raw, messy transcript. Filler words get tidied, punctuation finds its place, and your sentences come out ready to send.

Windows Setup, Step by Step

If you have never installed an open source app before, this walkthrough is for you. Follow the steps in order and you will be speaking in no time.

  1. Open your web browser. Any browser works — Edge, Chrome, Firefox, or whatever you already use every day.
  2. Go to the download link above. You will land on the flunkey releases page on GitHub.
  3. Look at the newest release at the top of the page. Releases are listed newest first, so the item at the top is the current version.
  4. Find the Windows file in the list of downloadable assets. It is labelled clearly as the Windows build.
  5. Click it to start the download. Your browser will show progress in the top-right corner or in your downloads list.
  6. When the download finishes, open it. Press Ctrl + J in most browsers to see your recent downloads, then click the file to open it.
  7. If Windows shows a blue "Windows protected your PC" box, click More info and then Run anyway. This is a standard safety prompt that appears for new and independently published software.
  8. Follow the prompts on screen. Accept the default options unless you have a reason to change them.
  9. Launch flunkey. Open it from the Start menu or from the shortcut that appears on your desktop.

That is it. There is no account creation step and no licence key to enter. The first time flunkey opens, it will guide you through a short setup so it can hear your microphone properly.

🎤 What You Can Do With flunkey

  • Dictate into any app. Email, chat, documents, forms, search bars, and note-taking tools all accept flunkey's output, because it types for you at the cursor.
  • Speak naturally. You do not need to talk like a robot. Pause, think, and carry on — flunkey keeps listening.
  • Get readable text. Smart clean-up removes the "umms" and "ahhs" and shapes your speech into sentences you would actually send.
  • Keep your hands on the keyboard. A quick shortcut starts and stops listening, so you never have to hunt for a button with the mouse.
  • Stay in your flow. flunkey sits quietly in the background until you need it, then gets out of the way again.
  • Own your setup. Because flunkey is open source, anyone can inspect how it works and suggest improvements.

💼 Everyday Workflows

The value of speaking instead of typing shows up fastest in repetitive writing. Answering a dozen short emails becomes a five-minute job. Replying to messages in a team chat stops interrupting your focus. Drafting a report outline while pacing the room is suddenly possible. If you have wrist strain or simply type slowly, flunkey removes a genuine daily obstacle.

Students use it for lecture notes. People with dyslexia use it to get ideas out of their heads without fighting spelling. Customer support staff use it to reply faster. Anyone who writes the same kind of message over and over will feel the difference within a day.

Making flunkey Feel Like Yours

flunkey ships with sensible defaults, but a few small adjustments make it feel personal. In the settings area you can choose which key combination starts and stops dictation, pick the microphone you want to use if your computer has more than one, and adjust how aggressive the text clean-up should be. Some people prefer a light touch that keeps their exact wording; others want a tidy, polished result.

Spend two minutes in settings before you start working. Choosing a shortcut that does not clash with the apps you already use is the single biggest quality-of-life improvement you can make on day one.

Getting the Best Results From Your Voice

Speech recognition is remarkably good, but a little care goes a long way. Use a headset or a microphone that sits close to your mouth rather than one across the room. Reduce background noise where you can — a fan, a television, or an open window will all compete with your voice. Speak at your normal pace instead of slowing down artificially, and say punctuation out loud only if you find it helpful.

The first few dictations may feel unusual. Most people find that after a day or two, speaking their thoughts becomes faster than typing them ever was.

🔒 Privacy and Your Voice Data

flunkey is open source, which means the code behind it is public and inspectable. That transparency matters for a tool that listens to your microphone. You control when flunkey is listening, and you can stop it at any moment with the same shortcut you use to start it. Clear, honest handling of your audio is a core part of the project's design, and the source is there for anyone who wants to verify it.

Troubleshooting the Basics

Nothing appears when I speak. Check that flunkey is running and that your microphone is selected in settings. Then click inside the text box you want to fill — flunkey types where the cursor is.

Windows blocked the app. This is the standard SmartScreen prompt for new software. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

The words are messy. Move your microphone closer, close background noise sources, and check the clean-up setting in the app.

I need a newer version. Return to the releases link and download the most recent entry at the top of the page.

Something still is not right. Open an issue on the repository and describe what happened. Screenshots help enormously.
